# The recipe, step by step

A complete example built this way, public under the MIT license: the promo video of SimplyBar,
<https://github.com/Iliesseu28/SimplyBar/tree/main/tools/promo-video>, with the renderer of its SwiftUI views in
`tools/screenshots/`. The template of this skill is the generic part of that project.

## 1. Copy the template

```sh
cp -R "<skill dir>/template" <app repo>/tools/promo-video
cd <app repo>/tools/promo-video && npm ci && npm run still    # renders out/still.png: the setup works
```

Set the output name, size, duration and poster time in `src/video.json`, the app's name, promise, store line and
colours in `src/brand.ts`. To write the final files elsewhere: `OUT_DIR=../../docs/video bash scripts/render.sh`.

| Generic: keep | Specific to the app: redo |
| --- | --- |
| `src/lib/camera.ts` (camera by anchors, log zoom) | `src/scene/layout.ts` (the stage, where each thing is, in points) |
| `src/lib/cursor.ts` (curved strokes, clicks, drags) | `src/scene/AppWindow.tsx` (the stand-in: replace with the app's views) |
| `src/lib/subtitles.ts`, `src/components/` | `src/scene/timeline.ts` (subtitle pages, gesture times), `src/scene/moves.ts` |
| `scripts/` (voice, words, sounds, render) | `src/narration.json` (the voice text), `src/brand.ts`, `src/video.json` |

## 2. Write the storyboard

See `storyboard.md`: 4 to 6 gestures, 60 to 70 words for 25 to 29 seconds, the richest image at the end (it
becomes the poster). The text goes into `src/narration.json`: voice, models, style, and lines with an `id` and a
`start` time. Show the storyboard to the user before recording anything.

## 3. Get the app's real views

Use images of the app itself, placed with `<Img src={staticFile("app/<name>.png")}>` at the rects of
`layout.ts`. Put them in `public/app/` (ignored by git when they are regenerated by a script).

- **Mac app (SwiftUI or AppKit)**: compile copies of the views into a tiny renderer outside the app and draw them
  offscreen (`NSHostingView` then `cacheDisplay(in:to:)`) at 4 pixels per point, with deterministic demo data.
  No screen recording permission is needed. A window that must look active (blue switches, selected rows): launch
  the renderer as a regular app (`open -W -n Renderer.app`, `LSUIElement` NO), bring the window to front, wait for
  `NSApp.isActive`, then draw `contentView.superview` (title bar included). Add corners, border and shadow in CSS.
  SimplyBar's `tools/screenshots/` does exactly this.
- **iOS app**: record the simulator (`xcrun simctl io booted recordVideo out.mov`) while a UI test plays the scene,
  or take screenshots in series from a UI test, then place them in a plain screen frame (no device bezel for an
  App Store preview).
- **Web app**: screenshots with Playwright at a fixed viewport and device scale factor 2.
- **What the operating system draws** (desktop, pointer, menu bar background, context menus, widget gallery) can
  be redrawn in CSS. Never draw a company logo, a dock, or the name of another app.
- **Live data**: pre-render a loop of readings (SimplyBar: 40), and pick one with the same clock everywhere
  (`state = floor(t / 0.5 s) % 40`) so the numbers agree between views.

## 4. Voice-over, then word times

1. `python3 scripts/voice.py` records each line (Vertex AI or Gemini API, see `tools.md`), retries a 429 after
   15 s times the attempt number, and writes each length to `src/voice.json`. Until then, lengths are estimated
   from the words, so the template renders without a voice.
2. `python3 scripts/words.py` prints when each word is said, in seconds from the start of the video.
   Whisper can misspell a word ("max" for "Mac's"): it only gives the times; the text comes from `narration.json`.
3. In `src/scene/timeline.ts`: subtitle pages cut where the voice breathes, the time of each gesture (`T`). Example:
   "CPU" at +0.92 s in a line that starts at 18.5 s, so the CPU widget lands at 19.4 s. The timeline throws an
   error if a line runs into the next one: move the next start, or record a shorter take.

## 5. Stage, camera, cursor

- **One continuous stage** (1440 x 810 points in 1920 x 1080, `K` = 4/3 pixels per point), filmed by one camera:
  the "scenes" are moments of the same stage. No CSS transition, no randomness.
- **Camera**: keyframes `{ t, zoom, ax, ay }`; `ax, ay` place the view in its margin (0 = against the left or top
  edge, 1 = right or bottom), and `anchorOn(point, zoom)` computes them to centre a point. Zoom interpolated on a
  log scale, easing `bezier(0.45, 0, 0.25, 1)`. Zoom only to make a small area readable, then give the overview back.
- **Cursor**: strokes `move(from, to, target, bend)`, a quadratic Bezier bent to one side (bend 0.04 to 0.18,
  alternate the sign), easing `bezier(0.3, 0.05, 0.12, 1)`. A click shrinks the arrow to 88 % for 2 frames and
  leaves a white ring growing where it landed. Drags: `press`, `move`, `release`.
- **Targets computed, never guessed**: click points come from `layout.ts`; in an image of the app, measure them on
  the PNG (pixels of the close button, of the switches), divided by the image scale.
- **Subtitles**: a pill `rgba(12,14,24,0.66)`, 44 px semibold, centred, 104 px above the bottom, one line
  (`nowrap`), 0.12 s fades; the opacity sits on the pill itself.
- **Sound**: one take per line, a synthesized click per click, a very quiet pad exactly as long as the video, then
  one loudness pass on the mix.

## 6. Check while editing

`npx remotion still Promo out/still.png --frame=<n>` every 2 to 3 seconds of video, shrunk to a small JPEG before
reading it. Look for: a target missed by the cursor, a subtitle over the thing that matters, an element cut by the
frame during a zoom, a gesture before its word. Count 5 or 6 rounds of fixes.

## 7. Render and verify

1. `bash scripts/render.sh`.
2. The checks of `tools.md`: duration, size, 30 fps, AAC stereo, about -16 LUFS, silences where expected.
3. Read the subtitles and the end card: no long dash, no other brand, no path of the machine, no price that dates.
4. Git: keep `public/voice/*.wav` and `takes.json` (a new take never sounds the same); ignore `public/app/`,
   `public/gen/` and `out/`.
5. Show the poster and the MP4 to the user; offer an App Store preview only after `app-store-previews.md`.

## 8. iPhone or iPad variant

For an App Store preview in portrait, set `width` and `height` in `src/video.json` (886 x 1920 for every current
iPhone, 1200 x 1600 for iPad), lay the stage out again in `layout.ts` (the stage becomes 664.5 x 1440 points on an
iPhone), keep subtitle pages short (they scale with the short side of the video), and use a full-screen view of the
app instead of a desktop.
